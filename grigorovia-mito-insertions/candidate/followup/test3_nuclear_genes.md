## Step by step

**1. *S. cerevisiae* queries, miniprot (the logged rule; `nuclear/pass1_calls.md`).**
- Complex III and IV subunits, heme a, cytochrome c, TUF1, MEF1, MSS51 and the ribosomal proteins were found in all
  six genomes. In *G. jiainica*, COX9 and QCR10 were missed.
- **PET309, CBP1 and CBS2 were not found in any *Grigorovia*.** PET309 and CBP1 were found in both controls, CBS2 in
  *K. africana* only.
- CBS1 was found in none of the six.

**2. Relatives' translations as queries (`nuclear/close_queries.md`).**
- DIAMOND (ultra-sensitive, frameshift-aware) finds PET309, CBP1 and CBS2 in **all four *Grigorovia***, at 24–32%
  identity to the relatives' proteins.
- The first pass had failed to detect them. These were not losses.

**3. Congener proteins as queries (`nuclear/congener_query_calls.md`, `nuclear/yak_queries_sources.tsv`).**
- 45 *G. yakushimaensis* proteins were used; none was available for CBS1, CBP2, PET54 or AEP2.
- *G. humatica* and *G. transvaalensis*: every gene present.
- *G. jiainica*: everything present except COR1 and PET309 ("broken", one frameshift each) and COX9, PET111 and PET122
  ("absent").

**4. Checked against the reads** (`nuclear/reads_check.md`).
- Data: the WGS run SRR16974510, from the same BioSample as the assembly. 86.0% of reads map to the assembly.
- Method: a read consensus was built with samtools, and the genes searched again in it.

## Heterozygosity: both copies checked (`nuclear/heterozygosity.md`)

**Genome-wide.**
- 25 largest contigs, 3,654,777 nt; 2,987,281 sites with 20–120 reads.
- **11.95 heterozygous-like sites per kb** (a second base in 25–75% of reads). Alternative-base fractions peak at
  0.4–0.5.
- Reads disagree fully with the assembly at only 0.007 sites per kb.
- This fits a heterozygous diploid, or higher ploidy. It also fits the fragmented assembly and its higher share of
  duplicated BUSCO genes (1.94%, against 0.82–0.95% in the congeners). The mitogenome is unaffected.

**Per gene.**
- Every panel gene was scanned for indels in ≥ 25% of reads and for stop-gains (single-base view).
- Each flagged spot was then resolved by grouping reads by the exact sequence they carry, using each read's alignment
  (`code/explore/read_haplotypes.py`), and translating each version in the gene's frame:

| gene | read versions (of spanning reads) | both open, in frame? | reads matching the assembly |
|---|---|---|---|
| COR1 | 18 / 5 (of 25) | yes (…IDKFDKIDSL… / …IDNANNKINKFDKIESL…) | 0 |
| PET309, repeat | 12 / 9 (of 23), differing by an in-frame 7-codon unit | yes | 0 |
| PET309, one-base "stop-gain" | 18 / 10 (of 36) | yes: a two-base change, Ser→Arg (QGNSNE / QGNRNE) | 10 |
| PET111 | 15 / 8 (of 26) | yes (poly-Asn, 0 or −6 nt) | 15 |
| PET494 (two spots) | 20 / 14 and 16 / 12 | yes (in-frame indels) | 20, 12 |
| ATP22, CYC3 | 7 / 6, 11 / 6 | yes (in-frame repeat indels) | 6, 6 |

**Final calls in *G. jiainica*: no broken copy.** Every callable gene keeps an open reading frame in both copies:
- COX1/COB activators PET309, MSS51, CBS2, CBP1;
- complex III: COR1, QCR2, CYT1, RIP1, QCR6–10;
- complex IV: COX4, COX5A, COX5B, COX6–9, COX12, COX13;
- heme a: COX10, COX15;
- cytochrome c: CYC1, CYC7, CYC3;
- mitochondrial translation: TUF1, MEF1 and 10 ribosomal proteins;
- CBP3, CBP6, PET111, PET122, PET494, ATP22.

**Not callable.**
- **CBS1:** found in none of the six genomes, by either method.
- **CBP2:** found in no *Grigorovia*, by either method; present in *K. africana*.
- **AEP2:** miniprot found it in none of the six genomes, the controls included. DIAMOND finds partial matches
  (coverage 0.67–0.68, 23% identity) in *G. jiainica* and *G. humatica*.
- **PET54:** miniprot found it only in *K. africana*. DIAMOND finds one weak partial match in *G. jiainica*
  (E 3e-4, coverage 0.45).
- CBP2 is needed in *S. cerevisiae* only to splice the cob bI5 intron, which no *Grigorovia* cob has.

## Rates (`nuclear/rates.md`)

**Identity to the *G. yakushimaensis* protein (median).**
- Activators and assembly factors: *G. jiainica* 43.1%, *G. humatica* 59.2%, *G. transvaalensis* 58.7%.
- Subunits, heme a, cytochrome c and translation: 72.3%, 80.6%, 79.7%.

**Reading.**
- *G. jiainica* is further from the congener in every group.
- Its distance ratio to *G. humatica* (−ln identity) is 1.61 for activators against 1.50 for subunits. So there is no
  clear excess on the activators.
- This is crude: no phylogeny, no dN/dS.

## Orthology (`nuclear/rbh.md`; added after the referee review)

- Each *G. jiainica* and congener protein was searched against the whole reviewed *S. cerevisiae* S288C proteome
  (6,733 proteins).
- For PET309, MSS51, CBS2, CBP1, PET111, PET494, ATP22, CBP3, CBP6, COR1, COX9, QCR10 and MEF1, the expected gene is
  the best hit in both species, and no other gene follows at E ≤ 1e-3 except MAS1 (after COR1) and MEF2 (after MEF1).
- **PET122 finds no *S. cerevisiae* hit at all.** Its orthology rests only on the relatives' queries (*K. africana*).
- Synteny was not checked.
- What these checks show: the machinery is present. They cannot show that Cox1 and Cob are made today.

## Read against the logged prediction

- **H2 predicted** losses or breaks, starting with the activators. **None were found, in either copy.**
- As logged, intact genes favour H1 only weakly, since a recent loss would not show yet.
- **False alarms on the way.** The logged first-pass rule would have reported PET309 and CBP1 lost across the genus,
  and PET309 and COR1 broken in *G. jiainica*. All of these were detection failures, assembly errors or an assembly gap.
